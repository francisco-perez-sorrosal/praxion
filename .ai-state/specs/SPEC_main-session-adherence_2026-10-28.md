## Feature Summary

Adds a second subject kind to the adherence eval, a rule move, measured in headless main sessions in full, lean and floor arms, so a rule can leave session-start residency only on evidence. Before this change the eval measured spawned roles only (the lean-spawn pilot and its continuation); Phase 3 of the startup-context diet needs to move always-loaded rules to other channels and had no instrument to show that the move loses nothing. Two facts shaped the design: a point-of-use reminder can arrive after the action it governs is composed, and a check that was never shown to fail proves nothing. So every governed check records whether the right text arrived and when (not only whether the rule was followed), and a rule-absent floor run on a handful of tasks proves each check can fail before any verdict is read.

The instrument is a sibling subpackage, `eval/src/praxion_evals/adherence/moves/` (about twenty modules, the largest under 800 lines), composing the role path's pure kernel (verdict over pairs, the infrastructure-stop streak, the lineage chain, the arm-copy diff, the atomic writer) after one behaviour-preserving refactor that surfaced `trailing_streak`, `chain_of` and the launch helpers. The entry parses `--role` or `--move` into a subject and dispatches; the role shell was not edited, and role runs, every results file recorded before this change and every finding of the kernel flip gate read as before. Move results are written to `.ai-state/eval_ledger/adherence/moves/` with their own index so the flip gate, its tests and the committed role index stay byte-identical. The arms are built from the runner's copy by applying only each arm's declared changes (a rule file, its delivery-manifest row, a hook registration and text, a resident kernel); the build refuses an undeclared or missing change naming the file and a lean declaration that cuts a line naming the line, and it checks offline that the Cursor rule export and the Codex rules bridge still reach every section they reach today. Channels are observed, not trusted: the arm build runs the session-start injection and each lean hook offline. A paired run is refused without a floor run that finished its plan, has the same floor identity and saw each governed check fail; allowances come from the run's own discordant count (`own-discordance-exact`), a coverage statement names the checks that could not hold to the margin, and a sensitivity line (the paired target times the floor's failure rate minus the full arm's) shows what a total loss of the rule would be expected to produce. `--dry-run` prices a move without a launch: 56 sessions at the defaults (48 paired over 24 tasks, 8 floor).

The first kit is the git-conventions move: a resident kernel of the lines a late reminder cannot repair (stage by name, review what is staged, no bare stash in a shared worktree, routine-on-clean is destructive-on-dirty, no AI authorship lines) with the rule's full text delivered before each staging or committing command; tasks over a managed and an unmanaged fixture with at least one untracked file the request does not name; and two mechanical governed checks, commit-message form and staging by name. The adr-conventions declaration builds its arms offline with no task kit (see Deferred at Close for what it cannot yet do).

Shape of the delivery: thirty-eight plan steps plus sub-steps. Eleven binding steps (test-engineer, driver files only) preceded every production step, so the outer loop was bound before any code; the production steps ran through the step-loop driver, with forced light reviews on the three steps carrying inferred load-bearing assumptions (the arm observation, the take proof and the trigger record). The final verifier's first pass read FAIL on the FC-01 measurement-log form only (prose in the `Reading` cells; the value held at 23,652 tokens), fixed by replacing the malformed rows. Fix steps 34c to 34e then aligned the authorship grader with the commit gate's definition and made it grade every stored line, with the gate's own `-m` gap filed as `td-517`. Step 37 recorded five probe sessions on the subscription (sonnet, $0.50 and six turns per session; $1.09 reported, $0 API, no judge) and froze three real 2.1.312 records; the fourth probe, the path-matched creating write, could not be recorded because the instrument cannot yet deliver that channel, which is what the deferral below records.

### REQ-01: A move is measured in the main session through the role machinery

**When** the operator runs the adherence entry naming a move instead of a role
**the system** runs each task as one headless main session whose own prompt is the task's request (no relay and no spawned role), pairs the full and lean sessions of each task, and records, verdicts, reports, stops, continues, closes, draws its mechanical allowances and lists earlier runs as it does for a role under the machinery lean-spawn-continuation landed (dec-462), with the move in the place of the role: the infrastructure streak counted per arm of the move; the move's identity, on which a continuation and an earlier run are matched, being the moved rule's text and every file each arm changes with its content; a fresh run of the move, floor or paired, refused while a stopped run of that move that was continuable when it stopped is neither continued nor closed; names the subject as a move in the results file and the report, never as a role; grades none of the role-only checks (C1 to C6 and the role rows) on a move session; leaves every role run, and every results file recorded before this change, reading as it does today; and changes no finding `python3 scripts/check_kernel_manifest.py` reports over any adoption ledger, and no role entry of `.ai-state/eval_ledger/adherence/index.json`, when a move run is recorded
**so that** a rule move is measured by the same instrument, under the same discipline, as a spawn flip, with no second verdict machinery to drift from the first, and the kernel flip gate reads exactly what it read before moves existed.

### REQ-02: Arms are declared changes to the runner's copy, and a move is a relocation

**When** a move's arms are built
**the system** builds each arm from the runner's own copy of the checkout by applying only that arm's declared changes (the rule file, its delivery-manifest row, a hook registration and its text, a resident kernel text), records the content digest of every declared change, refuses to run an arm whose copy differs from the checkout in any file outside its declaration or lacks any declared change (naming the file), never changes the checkout itself, and refuses a lean declaration in which any line of the moved rule is neither resident nor delivered by a declared channel (naming the line)
**so that** an arm differs from today in exactly the move and nothing else, and a move can relocate text but never cut it.

### REQ-03: An arm's take is proven from each session's own record

**When** a session of a move run is graded
**the system** reads that session's own record for every arrival of the moved rule's text at session start, and refuses to score, with the reason, a full session lacking the rule's text at session start, a lean session carrying the rule's full text at session start or lacking its declared resident kernel, and a floor session carrying any line of the moved rule; an unscored session enters no count; a lean session whose point-of-use or path-matched text never arrived is scored, because that is the trigger failure the run measures; and the proof asks the model to report nothing
**so that** every pair compares the arms it claims to, deterministically, without spending a session on proof.

### REQ-04: Each governed check records what arrived, when, and what it left

**When** a session of a move run is graded
**and** a governed check reads a governed action the session took
**the system** records, per governed check, each channel that carried the governing text into the session; whether it arrived; when, relative to the first governed action: before the action was composed, after it was composed but before it ran, after it ran, or never; the final-state outcome, graded on the task's end state; and the history outcome, graded on every governed action the session took: right first time, repaired (a violating action later brought into conformance), not repaired, or action never taken; reports a failure with no arrival as a trigger failure, apart from a failure with arrival; and records arrival as unknown, never as never arrived, when the session's record of arrivals is missing or unreadable
**so that** a reader can tell whether the right guidance arrived, and whether it arrived in time to shape the action or only to repair it.

### REQ-05: The verdict gates on final state, and claims bounded non-regression only

**When** a move run's verdict is computed
**the system** gates on the final-state outcomes of the governed checks alone, by the flip rule as dec-462 amends dec-459, with the move in the role's place and each mechanical allowance as REQ-15 states, counting complete pairs over distinct tasks; reports the history outcomes, the arrival timing by channel and the trigger failures per arm beside the verdict, never gating on them; and states that a clear verdict shows bounded non-regression within the margin and nothing about one channel beating another
**so that** a move clears only when its final effect holds, while the process evidence (late arrival, repair) stays on the record for the user to weigh.

### REQ-06: The unit of analysis is a distinct task, and a move governs one or two checks

**When** a move's task list is generated or its governed checks are declared
**the system** makes every task distinct in fixture, wording or both, and records each task's fixture, fixture kind and wording variant; counts a task at most once toward complete pairs and launches no second pair for it within a run beyond the declared infrastructure retry and a continuation's relaunch of a task whose sessions count as never launched (dec-462 clause 5); generates at least the declared target plus slack tasks (dec-459 defaults: 20 complete pairs plus a fifth, at least four); keeps every task's wording free of any statement or paraphrase of a governed rule line; refuses a move declaring no governed check or more than two; ties each governed check to the rule file and section it enforces, failing a test when that section is gone; and proves, with no session, that each governed check grades a planted violating end state as failed and a planted conforming one as passed
**so that** the pair count counts independent tasks, not repeated draws on one task, a task never hands the rule to the session it measures, and the false-regression burden of several checks stays bounded.

### REQ-07: A floor run proves each governed check can fail, and states what a total loss would show

**When** a move is to be measured
**the system** runs the floor arm on a declared handful of the move's tasks (default 8, at least 6) as a declared run of its own, before the paired run, under the stop, continuation and close every run has, its infrastructure stop continuable on its own streak because it has one arm; counts an errored floor session as no failure; refuses to launch a paired run that cites no floor run, cites one whose lineage stopped or was closed instead of finishing its plan, cites one whose identity differs (the floor arm's built copy, its tasks, the governed checks, the model and the declared settings; a change confined to the lean arm's declaration does not count, since the full and floor arms never load it), or declares a governed check that the cited floor run never failed on final state; resolves a citation of any part of a floor lineage to the whole lineage; lists floor runs apart from paired draws in every earlier-runs listing, as calibration runs are listed apart, naming every other floor run of the same floor identity beside the cited one; counts floor sessions in no paired count and no allowance; and reports, per governed check, the floor failures out of the floor sessions scored on it beside the full and lean failure counts, and a sensitivity line: the net discordance a total loss of the rule would be expected to produce at the paired target, estimated from the floor's failure rate against the full arm's, namely the declared target of complete pairs (20 at the defaults) times the difference between the floor's final-state failure rate on the check (the cited floor lineage's failures over its floor sessions scored on that check, an errored or unscored floor session in neither count) and the full arm's (its final-state failures over the check's usable pairs in this run, the pairs its verdict counts), shown to one decimal place beside the check's allowance in pairs (dec-462 clause 1, at this run's own discordant count), listing the check as not covered, and naming it in the clearance claim, when the unrounded figure does not exceed that allowance (a floor rate at or below the full arm's gives a figure at or below zero, so that check is not covered); the line is reported, never gated, and changes no verdict
**so that** before any verdict is read every check it rests on has been shown to fail when the rule is gone, by a floor run whose stopping was never chosen after its counts were seen, and a clearance never rests silently on a check that could not have seen the rule's total loss.

### REQ-08: Competing instructions are pinned identically in every arm

**When** a move run declares its settings
**the system** records before its first launch, and holds equal in every arm (floor included), the harness's commit-attribution setting, the model, the effort, the user instruction file each arm renders and every other always-loaded unit outside the move; and refuses a run whose arms would differ in any of them, naming it
**so that** a check measures the move, not a harness instruction that competes with the rule (such as the harness's own request to add an AI authorship trailer) or a difference the move did not make.

### REQ-10: Moves are measured where managed projects run

**When** a move's task list is generated
**the system** draws its tasks from at least two fixture kinds, a managed project (a project state directory and a project `CLAUDE.md` carrying the shipped blocks) and an unmanaged directory, at least a third of the tasks from each; starts every session in its task's fixture, never in the Praxion checkout; and reports per-kind counts beside the pooled verdict, which pools both kinds and is never gated per kind; a move that declares it changes delivery in one fixture kind only (such as project gating) draws every task from that kind, and the report names the restriction
**so that** a move is measured in the projects that will receive it, which carry more agents and may lag on Praxion features, not only in this repository.

### REQ-11: A move never leaves a host without hooks with less than today

**When** a move is declared
**the system** checks, before any session and over the lean arm's copy, that the Cursor rule export and the Codex rules bridge reach every section of the moved rule they reach over the checkout today, and that any text the lean arm delivers by a hook is read from a rule file those exports carry; where a host's export *carries* a rule file when the Cursor export writes it, or when the Codex bridge's manifest lists it with a load other than excluded (the bridge exports no rule text: its hooks point the session at the files its manifest lists, so the listed file in that tree is what reaches), and a section is *reached* on a host when every non-blank line the section holds in the checkout's rule file appears, unchanged, in a file that host's export carries; on Codex a section reached through an always-on listing today must still be reached through an always-on listing, since a move measured on Claude Code shows nothing about the bridge's path-matched timing; and refuses the move otherwise, naming the section and the host
**so that** with hooks absent behaviour falls back to today's, never to nothing.

## Key Decisions

Two ADR drafts landed (one architectural, one implementation), and the checkpoint decisions of the orchestrator and the user shaped the result (context copied from `LEARNINGS.md § Decisions Made`, `CHECKPOINT_DIGEST.md` and the draft ADR records).

**[systems-architect] A rule move is a second subject kind in a sibling subpackage over the adherence eval's shared kernel (`dec-draft-b9d1c991`, architectural)**: `eval/src/praxion_evals/adherence/moves/` composes the pure verdict, stop, lineage chain, arm-copy diff and atomic writer; the entry parses `--role` or `--move` into a subject and dispatches; the role shell is not edited. The component added is the `moves` subpackage. **Why**: REQ-01 needs role runs and the flip gate unchanged, and threading a subject through six files near 800 lines buys the role path nothing. **Alternatives**: a subject sum type through the role shell (rejected: a wire change and file-size ceilings). Risk accepted: two shells over one kernel, so a future cross-subject feature lands twice; drift between the two stop folds and lineage readers is closed by the shared `trailing_streak` and `chain_of` and a parity test each.

**[systems-architect] Move results live in their own directory and index (`dec-draft-dec75e6b`, implementation)**: `.ai-state/eval_ledger/adherence/moves/` with `moves/index.json`, regenerated by the same `--reindex`. **Why**: every role reader globs the role directory non-recursively, so the flip gate, its tests and the committed role index stay byte-identical and a move run in progress cannot make a gated commit stale. **Alternatives**: one index at schema 3; move standings under the role map.
