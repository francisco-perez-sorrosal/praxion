## Feature Summary

Finish the step-loop driver as a working loop: drive at least two of this pipeline's own implementer steps live through the real Agent tool; land the eight increments the previous pipeline deferred (iteration reporting in the spawn counter, in-loop light reviews, the step-loop procedure as a skill, the architecture documents, the turn-budget reminder and its live probe, the plan-local trigger prose and the loop pointers, the step-schema reading code brought under its size ceiling, and one count of an agent's distinct requests); and close the reminder's conditional decision with measured latency — so that the orchestrator's live context stops being the loop's bottleneck and the driver is the party that turns the crank in every Standard and Full pipeline.

## Acceptance Criteria

One criterion per surviving Key Signal of the task brief (S1–S3 and S5–S11; S4 is struck and its retirement is recorded in the Spec Notes), each traced to the requirements below; then the invariants; then the footprint tables. *The driver*, *the loop*, *a stop for a human* and *the cap* mean what the preamble of the Behavioral Specification below defines.

- [ ] **AC-01 (S1) Light reviews run inside the loop.** Over a plan whose verified step carries `review: force` or `tier: H` and does not carry `review: off`, `next` asks for that step's light review before any later step's request; an accept advances the loop; a revise yields a revision request for the same step; a second revise is a stop for a human; a review still marked unfinished is never read as an accept; and for no plan, history or review state does `next`, `record` or `status` exit 1 on a step marked for review (REQ-01, REQ-02).
- [ ] **AC-02 (S2) The turn-budget reminder, probed live and measured.** Hook tests over payload fixtures show one reminder per agent at 60 percent and one at 80 percent of its declared cap, none for the main session or for an agent with no declared cap, and a silent pass-through on every error path. One live probe of the registered reminder, in a session that loads this checkout's plugin (a nested session started for the purpose is enough; the orchestrator's own session is not restarted), shows the 60 and 80 percent lines reaching a capped subagent and nothing reaching the main session, and is recorded in `LEARNINGS.md` with the harness version, the command and what was seen; a probe the harness refuses is recorded there as a finding with its cause, never forced, and the criterion is then left to the verifier's judgment. FC-05 and FC-06 are read on the measuring machine and recorded in `MEASUREMENTS.md` and in the reminder's decision record, which is then either re-affirmed (both bounds hold) or has its reversal trigger applied (retired in favour of the static finish ordering, or a shell pre-filter shipped first when latency is the only failure), with the outcome recorded in `LEARNINGS.md` (REQ-03, REQ-04).
- [ ] **AC-03 (S3) The step-loop procedure is a skill.** `/praxion:step-loop <task-slug>` can be invoked by the user and by the model, acts on the task slug given to it, carries the five relay rules (pass the spawn request unchanged, call `record` only after the completion notification, relay the agent id and the marker, never edit the rendered prompt, never commit), and is named in the coordination protocol's "Plan ready" bullet; no command of that name exists (REQ-05, REQ-06).

## Requirements

The requirements describe the loop at its surface: the driver's command and its verbs, the pipeline documents it reads and writes, the Agent tool request the orchestrator executes, the reminder an agent sees, the counter's report, and the texts a reader follows. Five terms recur:

- *the driver* is the step-loop driver's command declared below; *the loop* is the span between the pre-mortem gate and the pre-verification checkpoint in which the driver selects, gates and commits each implementer step and the orchestrator only relays its requests;
- *the cap* is the attempt cap stated once in the completion-handshake procedure (two fresh attempts today);
- *a stop for a human* is a `needs-human` outcome with exit 2 that names the step and its cause;
- *the light-review trigger* marks a step that carries `review: force` or `tier: H` and does not carry `review: off`.

Requirement ids are this pipeline's own and appear nowhere in code, tests or docstrings; five requirements are inherited from the previous pipeline's archived specification, word for word except where the Spec Notes record a change.

### REQ-01: Light reviews run inside the loop

**When** a step verifies that the existing light-review trigger marks as risky or that carries `review: force`, and the step does not carry `review: off`
**the system** emits a light-review request for that step before any later step's request; an accept advances the loop, a revise yields a revision request for the same step, a second revise stops for a human, and a review still marked unfinished is never read as an accept
**so that** the bounded light review keeps its place at the step boundary when the driver turns the crank

### REQ-02: A step marked for review never breaks the driver

**When** `next`, `record` or `status` is called on a plan holding a step the light-review trigger marks, at any point of that step's attempts or review
**the system** answers with a spawn request, completion or a named stop, as it does for an unmarked step, and the marking never makes it fail with exit 1
**so that** a plan with a review-marked step can be driven past it, and anything the loop cannot resolve reaches a human as a named stop, never as an internal error

### REQ-03: A turn-budget reminder reaches the agent before its cap

**When** an agent running as a subagent with a declared `maxTurns` makes a tool call after its distinct API requests have crossed 60 percent, or 80 percent, of that cap
**the system** delivers, as `additionalContext` from a `PreToolUse` hook, one reminder per agent and threshold, stating the turns used of the cap and what to do (at 80 percent: finish the current edit, record results, stop in a committable state); it never fires for the main session or for an agent without a declared cap
**so that** an implementer learns it is near its cap while it can still stop in a committable state

### REQ-04: The reminder never gets in the way

**When** the reminder cannot read its input, the agent's transcript or the agent's declared cap, or fails in any other way
**the system** emits nothing, exits 0 and lets the tool call proceed; the hook is registered in `hooks/hooks.json`, so it runs in every session that loads the plugin
**so that** a defect in the reminder costs at most a missed reminder, never a blocked tool call

### REQ-05: Inside the loop the orchestrator only relays

**When** the orchestrator passes the pre-mortem gate of a pipeline whose plan has implementer steps
**the system** gives it a procedure that runs the loop by repeating `next`, executing each request through the Agent tool unchanged and calling `record` after each completion notification, and that forbids it, inside the loop, to compose an implementer prompt, read the rendered prompt, commit, or edit an `Attempts:` line
**so that** between the two checkpoints the orchestrator's role is relay only, and its context stays small by construction

### REQ-06: The relay procedure is a skill named where the steps start

**When** the user types `/praxion:step-loop <task-slug>`, or the model decides that a task's implementer steps should run through the loop
**the system** runs the step-loop procedure for that task slug, offered as a skill that both the user and the model can invoke and never as a command; the procedure states five relay rules — pass the spawn request to the Agent tool unchanged, call `record` only after the agent's completion notification, relay to `record` the agent id and the marker the orchestrator saw with the request id, never edit the rendered prompt, never commit — and the "Plan ready" bullet of `rules/swe/swe-agent-coordination-protocol.md` names it as the way implementer steps run
**so that** the relay procedure is found where the orchestrator decides how the steps run, under one name whoever starts it

### REQ-07: The budget procedure says how loop spawns count

**When** the orchestrator reads the spawn-budget procedure for a pipeline whose implementer steps the driver runs
**the system** states, in one authoritative place, how driver-emitted spawns are budgeted (as iterations, bounded by the plan-derived iteration budget), that quality bounds the loop (cap, then surface; never grind), and that cost is observed and recorded per pipeline, never capped in dollars; every other text that mentions the budget points to that place: the texts that mention the budget are the always-loaded coordination protocol, the loop's relay procedure and the texts a reader follows to run implementer steps; a mention is a paragraph of one of them that speaks of the budget together with iterations or the loop; and pointing is a link or a named reference to the authoritative place
**so that** the spawn budget and the attempt cap cannot contradict each other inside the loop

### REQ-08: The spawn counter reports loop agents as iterations

**When** `python3 scripts/spawn_count.py --slug <slug> --budget <n>` counts a task whose `ITERATION_LEDGER.jsonl` records agents under a driver request id
**the system** reports each such agent among the `iterations` and leaves it out of `charged` and of the budget verdict; an agent the driver requested that the ledger does not yet record still counts in `charged`; with no ledger, or a ledger whose records carry no request id, the report and the verdict are exactly what they are today; every existing reader of the counter's report, in either output mode, keeps working
**so that** the loop's attempts are bounded by the plan-derived iteration budget alone and never also charge the pipeline's spawn budget

### REQ-09: The architecture documents describe the loop as built

**When** a reader opens `docs/architecture.md`, `scripts/CLAUDE.md` or `.ai-state/DESIGN.md`
**the system** finds the step-loop driver and the turn-budget reminder described in each: in the developer guide as built components whose every named path exists; in the scripts guide as entries for the driver's command and the modules behind it; and in the design target with both rows of their section marked Built
**so that** a reader navigating the code or the design finds the loop that runs every Standard and Full pipeline, and the developer guide stays a subset of what the design target marks Built

### REQ-10: One light-review trigger, stated alike at every site

**When** a reader opens any of the four texts that state when an intra-step light review fires: `skills/software-planning/references/intra-step-review.md`, `agents/implementation-planner.md`, `skills/software-planning/SKILL.md` and the pipeline-rules row of `rules/swe/swe-agent-coordination-protocol.md`
**the system** states the same plan-local rule at each: a step carrying `review: force` or `tier: H` is reviewed unless it carries `review: off`; an uncertainty flag below 7 and a one-way door reach the trigger only through the planner marking the steps they bear on `review: force`; no site names either as a signal read when a step completes
**so that** the trigger the driver evaluates, the planner writes and the orchestrator reads is one rule, derivable from the plan alone

### REQ-11: The texts that run implementer steps point at the driver

**When** a reader follows the texts that describe running a Standard or Full pipeline's implementer steps: the attempt-cap paragraph of `skills/software-planning/references/agent-pipeline-details.md`, the plan and progress templates of `skills/software-planning/references/document-templates.md`, `agents/implementer.md` and `commands/resume-pipeline.md`
**the system** says that inside the loop the driver writes the `Attempts:` line ahead, commits a verified step and records the return, and that outside the loop the orchestrator does; the progress template's `Attempts:` example shows the request the driver names; the resume procedure hands a step whose `Attempts:` line names a request not yet recorded back to `next`, never to a fresh spawn by hand; the cap's value stays stated in one place
**so that** no text a reader follows contradicts the loop that actually turns the crank

### REQ-12: One count of an agent's distinct requests

**When** an agent's transcript is read to count its distinct API requests, by the context instrument's cap-out count, by the driver when it records the agent's turns, or by the turn-budget reminder
**the system** gives the same count for the same transcript in every reader, including transcripts with malformed, partial or repeated lines, from one definition of the count; a transcript a reader cannot parse yields no count from that reader rather than a wrong one, and the readers agree when they give the same count or when all of them give none
**so that** the turns the driver records, the threshold the reminder warns at and the cap-outs the instrument reports never disagree about the same agent

## Key Decisions

- **[systems-architect] The relay procedure is a skill (dec-437)**: `skills/step-loop/SKILL.md`, invoked as `/praxion:step-loop <task-slug>` by the user and the model.
  - Frontmatter: the slug arrives through `$ARGUMENTS`; narrow `allowed-tools`; a description of about 25 tokens.
  - The "Plan ready" bullet names it, and the driver's stop and handoff text names it.
  - No `commands/step-loop.md`.
  - Partial supersession of dec-432 (its command clause only).
  - **Why**: the user's 2026-10-07 ruling, and the live docs ("custom commands have been merged into skills"); dec-432's model-invocable clause still holds.
  - **Alternatives**: the command as dec-432 wrote it; prose read by path (no listing cost, but not discoverable).
- **[systems-architect] Split `_step_schema.py` with a mutation-line leaf behind a re-export (dec-435)**: `scripts/_mutation_grammar.py` takes the `Mutation:` line grammar, the tag-line parser and the one reading policy. `_step_schema` keeps document structure and re-exports every moved name. Partial supersession of dec-421's grammar-owner clause.
  - **Why**: tests reach names through `_step_schema`, so the only acyclic split that edits no test is a leaf below it. 821 → about 650 lines.
  - **Alternatives**: the results file against the plan and WIP readers (needs a third primitives module); a WIP-claims leaf (it would import its parent).
- **[systems-architect] One request count in the shared reader; newline-only JSON Lines; the instrument folded, with `--transcripts-dir` (dec-436)**: `hooks/_agent_transcript.request_count(reading)` is the one definition. The driver, the reminder and `context_baseline.py` read it. The instrument's own counter retires. `--transcripts-dir` is the BA-06 surface, with its default unchanged.
  - **Why**: one definition is required (REQ-12), and the design-time corpus check showed equal counts on 437/437 transcripts once the splitter is fixed.
  - **Alternatives**: two counters with an agreement test; a tolerant shared reader (it gives a plausible wrong count on a cut-off flush).
- **[systems-architect] dec-433 gets no new record now**: the hook is built as written (C6). C7 measures FC-05 and FC-06 and runs the nested live probe. Then the orchestrator either appends a dated in-force Status note with the readings (both bounds hold), adds the `sh` pre-filter as a new step and measures again (only latency fails), or retires the hook (needs the user's word and a spec amendment per Spec Note 3).
  - **Why**: the record's own status note assigns the disposition to the readings.
  - **Alternatives**: re-affirm or retire now (no evidence yet).
- **[systems-architect] Self-modification boundary for the driven band**: steps editing a module the driver imports while it gates (`_step_schema.py`, `hooks/_agent_transcript.py`, the `_step_loop_*` state, action and record modules) run by hand before the band. The band drives C6 (first; `review: force`, so its in-loop review is REQ-01 live) and C3. C3's text-constant edit in `_step_loop_render.py` is the one named exception, guarded by the render suite.
  - **Why**: a driver gating the step that edits it can fail as exit 1, mid-probe.
  - **Alternatives**: drive the schema split or the counter fold (they test self-modification, not the loop).
- **[systems-architect] Smaller shapes (implementation)**:
  - the counter's `iterations` is a list of objects with `agent_id`, present only when non-empty;
  - a review's ledger record copies the reviewed record's verdict and evidence, with `commit: null` and no new key;
  - `parse_request_id` lives beside `RequestKey` in the renderer (the state module's import allowlist forbids importing it, so its kind regex stays);
  - the reminder's checks run cheapest first, and the 80 % marker short-circuits before any transcript read;
  - FC-06 uses a real implementer transcript cut to 100 requests, with a fresh session per timed run.
- **[implementation-planner] Fifteen steps in three bands; the verifier is outside the plan.** Hand steps 1-6 (baseline, spawn counter, schema split, the BA-06 binding, one request count, in-loop review), the driven band 7-12 (reminder hook, relay skill, the orchestrator's probe, trigger sites, loop pointers, architecture documents), the orchestrator's closing steps 13-15. The verifier follows the pre-verification checkpoint, so it is not a step (a verifier step would make `next` stop `not-driven` where the plan is complete). **Why**: the order is the architect's § Step Order, kept. **Alternatives**: a verifier step (rejected: it sits behind a user checkpoint); splitting C5 into a reader step and an instrument step (rejected: the brief names C5 as one heavy step alone; the step block orders the reader half first so a cap-out remainder is the instrument half).
- **[implementation-planner] The "Plan ready" bullet keeps "run tests --> fix cycle if needed"** (context review item 9) where the design's C3 row drops it. **Why**: the phrase still holds at the post-loop integration checkpoint and for Direct and Lightweight work, it costs about +7 tokens of the +100 headroom, and the oracle needs only `step-loop` on the line. The edit is reversible inside step 8. **Alternatives**: the design's drop (byte-neutral).
- **[implementation-planner] The skill's `allowed-tools` omits `Read`** (context review item 5) where I-3 lists it. **Why**: the relay rules forbid reading the rendered prompt, and the grant only pre-approves, so the narrower grant costs nothing. **Alternatives**: I-3's list.
- **[implementation-planner] Architecture documents are one step (12), after the reminder decision's disposition.** No `[Architecture]` tag on earlier steps. **Why**: the developer guide lists built components and checks that every path it names exists, so earlier tags would force edits before the code exists; the orchestrator's disposition makes this the last production step.
- **[implementation-planner] No `mutation:` tags.** Steps 2, 5 and 7 touch or import `hooks/`, which the sensor's flat sandbox cannot import; step 3's leaf is exercised through suites that shell out to git; the rest are prose or hook-registration steps.
- **[implementation-planner] Two steps carry `review: force` (6 and 7); no step carries `tier: H`.** Step 6 is the orchestrator's recommendation (reviewed outside the loop, because the in-loop review it builds does not exist yet); step 7 carries Uncertainty Flag 6/10 on transcript flush and its in-loop review is the live run of REQ-01. Step 3 (13 importers) is guarded by the unedited suites rather than a review. **Alternatives**: `tier: H` on steps 3 and 5 (rejected: each extra review is a charged spawn out of an envelope that already reads 13 of 32 and the guards are executable).
- **[implementation-planner] The FC-10 budget the driver derives is 20**: nine implementer steps times the cap of two, plus one for each of the two review-marked steps; the by-hand steps are counted too because the reader counts every implementer step of the plan.
- No ADR fragment was authored: none of these decisions adds, removes or moves a component, or changes a boundary (the architect's three drafts carry the architectural ones).
