## Feature Summary

Before this change the log's rotation kept exactly one archive and `os.replace` destroyed the previous one (`dec-250`), so history reached back about seven weeks and the next rotation erased them in one call; `dec-377`'s own reversal trigger named exactly that loss. Every pipeline runs in a worktree whose log main never saw: ten worktrees held about 4,650 rows of spawns, stops, costs and gate fires that `git worktree remove` would take with them, `/merge-worktree`'s "reconcile the log" step was inert, and the spawn counter undercounted split pipelines (td-308). Nobody checked whether the log itself was whole: the September defects (rows dropped from a subdirectory cwd, hooks silently off) were found by accident inside pipelines.

The retention policy now lives in one place, `hooks/_observation_log/retention.py` (size cap, archive count 5, a 26-week history target), and rotation shifts numbered archives by rename inside the writer's lock, filling a gap before dropping the oldest, so no row is ever read or rewritten and the legacy `.1` survives as position 2. The reader's `segment_listing` is the only archive discovery: every history reader (the recovery window, the cost collector, the spawn counter, merge-in, the health family) inherits the policy, names an unreadable or missing archive, and never builds an archive path by hand. Discovery is bounded by the policy count, so a backup kept beside the log under a numeric suffix is read as history and reported by P09 rather than exploding the listing.

Merge-in is a copy path in the owner package (`checkouts.py`, `merge_in.py`): a worktree's rows the main log lacks are appended through the writer's lock, deduplicated on whole-row identity, unchanged (`project` and `log_mode` kept), repeatable without duplicates, with the worktree's own log left intact. It runs from `/merge-worktree` step 9.5 before the worktree is removed and from the post-merge finalize chain (`scripts/finalize_chain.sh` step 1.5 through `scripts/merge_worktree_log.py --merged`) for every worktree whose branch *this* merge newly brings in -- a ruling the Step 8 light review forced, so a running pipeline's half session is never copied -- and it resolves the recording mode key by key from the project's settings over the shell, the way a session sees it. The chain step reaches every onboarded project's hooks at the next release (user decision SQ-1). Order-dependent consumers (the spawn counter, P03, the reconciler) now stable-sort by recorded time, so verdicts do not depend on when a row arrived, and the spawn counter reads every checkout.

The sentinel's Pipeline Discipline dimension gains rows P09–P14 (`scripts/check_observation_log_health.py`), registered in the catalogue, the dispatch table and the row-contract test, each with a canary and a golden bad-case: archive coverage against the policy, rotation state, malformed lines per segment, helper share, the distribution of `log_mode_source` (the field `dec-401` handed off, now consumed), and worktree logs never merged -- including a worktree whose log cannot be read whole, named with its reason. A missing, unreadable or empty log is one of the three named skips, never a clean pass. Every archive is ignored by version control here and in onboarded or upgraded projects, and the live evaluation treats every file beside the log as a hook byproduct.

Two rework points shaped the result. The Step 8 light review refuted the post-merge step's early-copy premise (a worktree with no commits counted as contained, so a running pipeline's agent starts reached main's log without their stops and P03 warned permanently); the architect ruled that the chain copies only the worktrees *this* merge brings in and honours the project's recording setting, applied and re-reviewed (Steps 8a–8c). The final verification failed on a stale footprint reading and on P14 collecting a worktree's unreadable segments without warning -- an unsurfaced "every reader already degrades" assumption from Step 8b; the architect's second ruling (Step 15a) fixed P14 and P11, bounded archive discovery to the policy count, made the chain resolve the recording mode key by key as Claude Code does, and named rather than built the post-merge gap for merges finished outside `git merge`; the re-verification passed with findings.

## Requirements

Verbatim from `SYSTEMS_PLAN.md § Behavioral Specification` as extracted by `extract_spec.py` (digest `9d54627f40c8`; REQ ids are pipeline-local and appear nowhere in code, tests or docstrings). The extract's preamble defines the terms: the **log** is a checkout's `observations.jsonl`; an **archive** is a rotated-out copy kept beside it; a **segment** is the active log or one archive; the **main checkout** is the repository's primary working tree and a **worktree** any other; the **retention policy** states how many archives are kept and the span they cover; **merge-in** copies a worktree's rows into the main checkout's log; two rows are **equal** when they hold the same fields with the same values.

### Retention and rotation

### REQ-01: A rotation keeps earlier archives

**When** a hook appends a row to a checkout's log and the active log has reached its size cap
**the system** moves the active log into the newest archive position and shifts every existing archive one position older, removing an archive only when the retention policy's archive count, which is more than one, would otherwise be exceeded, and then only the oldest
**so that** a rotation never destroys history the policy promises to keep, while the log and its archives stay within a fixed disk bound.

### REQ-02: The archive written before this change is kept

**When** the first rotation under the retention policy happens in a checkout that already holds the single archive `observations.jsonl.1` written by the previous scheme
**the system** keeps that archive's rows unchanged as the next-older archive, and every history reader keeps reading them
**so that** the weeks of history it holds today survive the change instead of being erased by the next rotation.

### REQ-03: A rotation that fails part-way loses no row

**When** a rotation cannot complete — a rename fails part-way through the shift, or the state directory refuses the change
**the system** still records the row being appended, leaves every earlier row in exactly one segment, and completes the shift at a later rotation
**so that** a filesystem fault costs at most a delayed rotation, never a lost row or a lost archive.

### REQ-04: Concurrent appends at the cap rotate once

**When** two or more hooks append at the moment the active log reaches its size cap
**the system** rotates once and records each appended row exactly once, whole, in one segment
**so that** concurrent sessions never split, duplicate or drop a row across a rotation.

### REQ-05: Recording below the cap costs what it cost before

**When** a hook appends a row and the active log is below its size cap
**the system** does no file work it did not do before this change — it opens no archive, lists no directory and starts no process — and a rotation at the cap renames files without reading or rewriting any row
**so that** retention adds no work to the per-tool-call recording path.

### History readers

### REQ-06: History readers reach every retained archive in time order

**When** a reader of the whole history — the spawn-budget counter or the project-metrics cost report — reads a checkout's log
**the system** gives it every retained archive and the active log, oldest first
**so that** a spawn first recorded in the oldest retained archive and resumed in the active log counts as one spawn and one resume, and an agent cost recorded in any retained archive is counted.

### REQ-07: The recovery window keeps its meaning

**When** pipeline recovery reads the log with its age window (seven days unless the caller sets another)
**the system** gives it every row whose recorded time falls inside the window, whichever segment holds it, the active log included, and no row whose recorded time is older than the window; a row in the active log whose time cannot be read is still given, because it may belong to the session in progress, while such a row in an archive is not
**so that** several rotations within one window cannot hide a recent agent stop, and neither longer retention nor rows merged in from a worktree widen what recovery considers.

### REQ-08: Active-only and tail readers see what they saw before

**When** a reader that reads only the active log, or only its tail, reads a log after this change
**the system** gives it, for the same log contents, exactly the rows it was given before this change and no archive row
**so that** the calibration reminder, the session summary, the handoff readiness gate, the lifecycle-pairing check and the other active-only readers keep their cost and their verdicts.

### REQ-09: An unreadable or missing archive is named, never taken for the end of history

**When** a retained archive cannot be read, or a position in the archive sequence is missing between two present archives
**the system** still reads every other segment, each history reader names the unread or missing archive in its output, and the spawn-budget counter does not report its tally as complete, as it already refuses to for an unreadable segment
**so that** a damaged archive degrades a verdict visibly instead of silently shortening history.

### Worktree merge-in

### REQ-10: The merge command merges the worktree's log before removing it

**When** `/merge-worktree` merges a worktree's branch into the main checkout
**the system** appends to the main checkout's log, before the worktree is removed, every row of the worktree's log — its active log and every archive — that the main log does not already hold, and reports how many rows it copied and skipped; a worktree with no log has nothing to merge, which is not a failure, and when the log mode in effect is `off` the merge-in appends nothing and says so
**so that** the spawns, stops, costs and gate fires a pipeline recorded outlive its worktree.

### REQ-11: A merge outside the command merges the log too

**When** the main checkout's branch comes to contain the branch of a worktree that still exists, through a merge or a pull run in the main checkout without `/merge-worktree`
**the system** performs the same merge-in as the command does, at that merge
**so that** a pipeline merged through a plain merge or a pull request does not leave its rows in a worktree that is later removed.

### REQ-12: Merged rows keep their identity and mode

**When** a row is copied from a worktree's log into the main log
**the system** writes it with exactly the fields and values it carried, so its `project` still names the worktree it was recorded in and its `log_mode`, or the absence of one, is unchanged
**so that** readers that attribute rows by checkout, such as the spawn-budget counter for rows recorded before spawns carried a slug, attribute merged rows exactly as before, and no merged row is read under a different mode.

### REQ-13: Merge-in is repeatable without duplicates

**When** merge-in runs again for a worktree whose rows were merged before, including after the main log has rotated since
**the system** skips a worktree row only when the main log, counting every retained archive, already holds a row equal to it, and appends every other row
**so that** re-running a merge, or merging a worktree that kept recording after an earlier merge, never duplicates a row and never drops one of two distinct events that differ in any field, such as the agent, the tool, the gate or the time.

### REQ-14: The worktree's own log is left intact

**When** merge-in has copied a worktree's rows
**the system** leaves the worktree's active log and every archive byte-for-byte unchanged, and the worktree keeps recording to its own log until it is removed
**so that** a worktree stays usable after merge-in and a failed or repeated merge loses nothing at the source.

### REQ-15: Merge-in and hooks share the main log safely

**When** a hook appends to the main log while merge-in is copying rows into it, or the copied rows carry the main log past its size cap
**the system** records every hook row and every merged row exactly once and whole, and applies the size cap and the retention policy to merged rows as to any other append
**so that** merging never corrupts the main log or bypasses its rotation.

### REQ-16: Verdicts do not depend on when a row arrived

**When** the spawn-budget counter, the lifecycle-pairing check or pipeline recovery reads a log in which merged rows were appended after rows that are later in time
**the system** reaches the same verdict as it does for the same rows written in time order
**so that** merge-in changes what the main log holds without changing what any verdict means.

### REQ-17: A log that cannot be merged degrades visibly

**When** a worktree's log cannot be read, holds malformed lines, or the main log cannot be written during merge-in
**the system** completes the code merge unaffected, copies every row it can read, skips malformed lines, reports the reason with the counts of copied, skipped and malformed rows, and does not remove the worktree while its log still holds rows the main log lacks unless the user chooses to discard them
**so that** a log fault never blocks a merge and never silently destroys a pipeline's record.

### Cross-checkout consumers

### REQ-18: The spawn tally spans the repository's checkouts

**When** the spawn-budget counter tallies a slug
**the system** counts the slug's spawns recorded in the main checkout's log and in the log, archives included, of every worktree of the repository that still exists, counts each spawn once even when merge-in has copied its row into a second log, and names in its verdict every one of those logs it could not read instead of reporting the tally as complete
**so that** a pipeline whose early spawns ran from another checkout, or whose earlier worktree was merged and removed, is charged for every spawn it made.

### REQ-19: The cost report counts each agent once across checkouts

**When** the project-metrics cost report reads the main checkout and the repository's worktrees after one or more worktrees were merged in
**the system** counts each agent's cost once, whether its row sits in a worktree's log, in the main log, or in both, and keeps counting a merged worktree's costs after that worktree is removed
**so that** merge-in neither inflates the cost record nor, after cleanup, erases part of it.

### Log health

### REQ-20: The log-health check runs in the sentinel's automatic pass

**When** the sentinel's automatic check families run, including through `python3 scripts/run_check_families.py --table`
**the system** runs the log-health checks over the log of the checkout it runs in and reports each finding with the check it belongs to, a severity, the entity it concerns (a segment, a session or a worktree) and a message, together with the counts it examined, the retention policy (archive count and history target) and the worktree age limit it judged against
**so that** the log's health is audited on every run instead of being discovered by accident inside a pipeline.

### REQ-21: Archive coverage is judged against the policy

**When** the log-health check examines a log's archives
**the system** reports the retained history span (oldest and newest row times) and the archive count as information, and reports a warning when a position in the archive sequence is missing between two present archives, when more archives exist than the policy keeps, or when the archive count has reached the policy's count while the retained span is shorter than the policy's history target
**so that** a lost archive, unenforced retention, and a policy the log's volume has outgrown each surface as a named finding.

### REQ-22: Rotation state is checked

**When** the log-health check examines the active log
**the system** reports its size against the size cap as information, and reports a warning when the active log has grown past the cap by more than a single append
**so that** a rotation that has stopped firing is seen before the active log grows without bound.

### REQ-23: Malformed lines are reported per segment

**When** a segment of the log holds lines that are not a single JSON object
**the system** reports a warning for that segment naming it, the count of malformed lines and their line numbers, and still judges its well-formed rows
**so that** a writer or merge defect that corrupts rows is caught where it happened.

### REQ-24: Helper share is reported as information

**When** the log-health check examines the log's stop rows
**the system** reports, as information and never as a warning, how many stops were harness helper stops, counting older stop rows that self-report as helper calls, how many were real agent stops, and the share helpers form of all stops (helper stops plus real agent stops)
**so that** an attribution figure computed over stops can be checked against how many of those stops were helpers.

### REQ-25: Sessions recorded under an unusual mode setting are surfaced

**When** the log-health check examines the log's session-start rows
**the system** reports as information the count of sessions per recorded `log_mode_source` value and the count recorded before modes existed (no mode source); reports a warning for each session recorded with `invalid-setting`, naming the session; and reports a warning for any row recorded with `legacy-disable`, a setting under which nothing is recorded
**so that** a mistyped `PRAXION_OBSERVATION_LOG` that silently fell back to full recording, or a row that contradicts the mode rules, is noticed.

### REQ-26: Worktree logs left unmerged are reported

**When** the log-health check finds a worktree of the repository whose log holds rows that have no equal row in the main checkout's log
**and** the worktree's newest row is older than the age limit the check states
**the system** reports a warning naming the worktree, its count of unmerged rows and the time of its newest row, while a worktree whose newest row is within the age limit is reported as in flight, as information
**so that** a pipeline's record is rescued by a merge-in before its worktree is removed, and active pipelines raise no warning.

### REQ-27: A missing or empty log is a named skip, never a clean pass

**When** the log-health check runs in a checkout whose log is absent, present but unreadable in every segment, or readable but holding no rows
**the system** skips each check that reads that log with exactly one of `substrate absent`, `reader unreachable` or `substrate carries no history` respectively, still runs the unmerged-worktree check when worktree logs exist, and, when only some segments are unreadable, reports each one as a warning and judges the rest
**so that** an audit never reports a log as healthy when it could not look at it, and every skip names its remedy.

### Ownership, version control and the live evaluation

### REQ-28: Every read and write goes through the log's owner

**When** any part of this change reads or writes the log, its archives or a worktree's log
**the system** does so only through the component that owns the log, so the consumer-contract, writer-truth and private-reader gates pass with every new reader declared, and each gate still fails on its canary
**so that** a later retention change reaches every reader by changing one place, and the single-owner guarantee stays enforced rather than assumed.

### REQ-29: Archives stay out of version control everywhere

**When** a checkout holds archives beyond the first, in this repository or in a project set up or upgraded by onboarding
**the system** has version control ignore every archive, as it ignores the log and `observations.jsonl.1` today
**so that** the log stays out of version control as already decided, and no onboarded project is offered archives as untracked files to commit.

### REQ-30: The live evaluation treats archives as hook byproducts

**When** the live evaluation compares a sandbox's files before and after a scenario
**the system** treats every file the log keeps beside itself, every archive included, as a hook byproduct
**so that** retention never makes a scenario fail its file-change check.

## Key Decisions

One ADR landed (`dec-424`, architectural), one user decision at intake, and five architect decisions in `SYSTEMS_PLAN.md § Decisions`, two of which were amended by rulings at the two rework points (context copied from there and from `LEARNINGS.md § Decisions Made`).

**[main-agent] User decision SQ-1, 2026-10-03 -- ship REQ-11**: a merge or pull in the main checkout without `/merge-worktree` merges a still-existing worktree's log too, through the post-merge finalize chain. **Why**: the pipeline exit procedure allows plain merges and PR merges, which the command alone would miss; the fleet reach of a hook step was named and accepted.

**[systems-architect] D1 -- Numbered archives shifted in the lock, count-bounded (`dec-424`)**: positions `.1 … .5` shifted by rename with gap fill; timestamped names, one growing archive and compression were rejected (a gap would be inexpressible, or O(size) work under the lock that `dec-250` already refused). **Why**: history grows from about 7 to 35–70 weeks with a fixed disk bound and no row ever read or rewritten; the legacy `.1` becomes position 2 unchanged.

**[systems-architect] D2 -- Merge-in is a copy path in the owner package keyed by whole-row identity (`dec-424`)**: reading live worktrees in place cannot outlive `git worktree remove`, and the reconciler's `timestamp|session_id|event_type|tool_name` key folds distinct `agent_start` rows and gate fires. **Why**: rows are copied unchanged, so whole-row identity is exact. *Amended at Step 15a (W-8)*: the main identity index is read outside the writer's lock; the rotation/concurrency race can add one extra copy of a held row and never lose one, accepted as td-330 rather than stalling every hook for the ~1 s index read.

**[systems-architect] D3 -- Trigger = `/merge-worktree` plus a state-driven post-merge step (`dec-424`)**: state-triggered like the finalize chain itself, so fast-forward merges, merge commits and pulls are covered without parsing which branch merged. *Amended at Step 8a*: the chain copies only worktrees *this* merge brings in (HEAD contained in main's HEAD and not in `ORIG_HEAD`), because copying a contained worktree with no commits of its own while its sessions run put agent starts into main's log without their stops and made P03 warn permanently; the chain honours the project's recording setting. *Amended at Step 15a (W-1, W-9)*: a merge git finishes outside `git merge` (a conflict or `--no-commit` completed by `git commit`) and a pull that rebases local commits run no post-merge hook -- named, not built; a post-commit/post-rewrite trigger is the user's decision, recorded as the ADR's reversal trigger -- and the mode keys resolve key by key, settings over the shell, as Claude Code applies them.

**[systems-architect] D4 -- The log-health family is rows P09–P14 of Pipeline Discipline (no ADR)**: the P preamble already defines the substrate and the three named skip states; a new dimension would add a scoring row and a report section for six checks over one substrate. *Amended at Step 15a (FAIL-2)*: P14 reads a worktree when its listing has a segment or an error and names a worktree whose log cannot be read whole (`unjudged`, under the shared age gate); P11 warns when the state directory cannot be listed for archives; archive discovery's `missing` is bounded to the policy count and a surplus position is P09's to report.

**[systems-architect] D5 -- Order-dependent consumers order rows by recorded time (no ADR)**: merge-in and cross-checkout reading break file order, so each consumer stable-sorts by `row_time` rather than merge-in rewriting the main log in time order under the lock.

**Declared limits** (`SYSTEMS_PLAN.md § Declared limits (post-implementation)`): a state directory that can be entered but not listed is named by merge-in and the health family but still reads as "no archives" in the spawn counter, the recovery reader and the cost collector (td-326); the post-merge gap for merges finished outside `git merge` (above); the identity-read race (td-330); after the archive count is lowered, a deleted run of in-policy positions below a surviving surplus position is not reported as missing, though P09 still warns on the surplus.

**Follow-up.** The post-merge gap this spec declared (a merge git finishes outside `git merge`, or a rebasing pull) is closed by `SPEC_merge-triggers_2026-10-03.md` (`dec-425`, partially superseding `dec-424`): post-commit and post-rewrite triggers; a squash merge and a rebase that rewrites no commit remain the named limits.

**Second half of `wal-modes`.** With this spec the effort the 2026-09-26 brief described is complete: `wal-modes-core` gave the log one owner and three recording modes; `wal-retention` gave it history, cross-checkout reach and a health check. Follow-ups are ledger rows, not a third pipeline.
