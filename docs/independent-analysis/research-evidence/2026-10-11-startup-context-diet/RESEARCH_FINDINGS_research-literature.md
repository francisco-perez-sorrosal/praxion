Appraisal applied: `evidence-appraisal` six-question check (design vs claim, setting transfer, drift, review status, incentive, warrant). Where a row says "abstract only", the numbers come from the abstract and the full tables were not read.

## Research Goal

Establish what peer-reviewed, preprint and vendor research (2023 → Oct 2026) says about how the **size and composition of resident context** (always-loaded rules, CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md, skill listings, preloads) affects LLM-agent quality and cost, and which techniques for keeping it small have empirical support — appraised for whether each source actually licenses the claim Praxion would make.

## Research Questions

1. Does resident length degrade quality even when the relevant information is present, and how do plausible-but-irrelevant distractors behave — specifically for 60–90K tokens of mostly-irrelevant guidance on frontier models?
2. How many simultaneous instructions do models follow; primacy/recency; rule conflicts — and what does that imply for ~25K tokens of always-on rules?
3. Do repository context files (AGENTS.md / CLAUDE.md / cursor rules) help or hurt coding-agent success and cost? Human vs generated? Size effects?
4. What is the evidence for selecting guidance just-in-time (skill libraries, skill retrieval, procedural memory, evolving playbooks, triggered injection)?
5. What compression results transfer to *instructions/rules*; any "less is more" pruning results?
6. What does the evidence say about sub-agent context isolation (token cost vs quality; what a sub-agent should inherit)?
7. What is the accepted survey-level taxonomy?

Scope boundary: research literature and vendor research reports. Claude Code harness mechanics (what the harness can and cannot defer) are out of this lens except where a paper measured them. No Praxion code was examined beyond the brief and the prior spike's TL;DR.

## External Findings

### Q1 Resident context length and distractors

- **Length alone costs quality — established mechanism, unmeasured magnitude at Praxion's operating point.** Du et al. (EMNLP 2025 Findings) and Levy et al. (ACL 2024) are independent traditions showing degradation with input length even when the needed information is present and retrieval is perfect; NoLiMa (ICML 2025) shows non-literal lookups collapse by 32K on 2024–25 models. Praxion's case is non-literal: a rule must be *recognised as applicable*, not string-matched. [certainty: high for the mechanism — three peer-reviewed groups; low for magnitude at 60–90K on 2026 Opus/Sonnet — none of them tested those models at that length]
- **Distractor composition matters more than raw size.** Chroma (18 models, incl. Claude 4) finds a single near-miss distractor already hurts; Gao et al. (2026) find the damage is front-loaded — the first small fraction of hard distractors does most of it, and **filtering helps mostly by shortening**. Li (2026) finds skill-selection decay driven by semantic confusability. For Praxion, overlapping, similar-sounding rules and skill descriptions are the "hard distractor" class. [certainty: med — Chroma is vendor research, Gao and Li are preprints; directions agree]
- **Counter-evidence on frontier robustness.** Eliav (2026) reports retrieval recall near ceiling through 64–128K on five (unnamed) models; Martin & Roger (2026) only see large drops for Opus 4.6 at 800K. Together they suggest pure *lookup* in Praxion's 60–90K band may be nearly free on 2026 models; the open question is reasoning-while-distracted, which Du/NoLiMa/Chroma say degrades earlier. [certainty: low — no study measures guidance-prefix + agentic coding at 60–90K]
- **Claude-specific failure shape:** Chroma reports Claude models respond to long, ambiguous context by **abstaining** rather than hallucinating (largest focused-vs-full gap). In an agent this would surface as hedging/under-acting, consistent with Xia et al.'s "early give-up" under accumulated context. [certainty: low — two sources, different settings]

### Q2 Instruction density

- **Joint compliance falls multiplicatively with the number of instructions that must all hold at once.** IFScale (NeurIPS 2025), ManyIFEval (EMNLP 2025 Findings), Eliav (2026) and Anand & Chattaraj (2026) converge: per-instruction accuracy stays high, but the probability that *all* are satisfied collapses (Claude 3.5 Sonnet 0.95 → 0.48 at 10; Sonnet 4 94.4% keyword coverage at 100 → 42.9% at 500; perfect response 0 by 80 rules). Primacy bias appears at mid-density (~150–200). [certainty: high — four independent groups]
- **Transfer caveat (load-bearing):** every one of these benchmarks makes *all* instructions apply to *every* output. Praxion's ~25K tokens of always-on rules are overwhelmingly conditional (commit rules apply at commit time, ADR rules when writing ADRs). The quantity these papers license Praxion to worry about is **concurrently-applicable instructions per action**, which no study has measured for a conditional rulebook. [certainty: high that the caveat holds — it follows from the designs]
- **Conflicts, not count, drive the collapse.** Anand & Chattaraj attribute the drop to reproducible pairwise conflicts; Control Illusion (AAAI 2026) shows conflicts are resolved by model priors and social cues, not by role or position. A large rulebook with latent contradictions is therefore resolved unpredictably. [certainty: med]
- **Unnecessary instructions are not inert.** Qi et al. (2026) show that adding constraints the correct answer *already satisfies* still reduces task performance (incl. Claude Sonnet 4.5); Gloaguen et al. show agents act on context-file instructions (uv: 1.6 uses/task vs <0.01), adding steps and cost. [certainty: med-high — two independent groups, different methods]
- **The one Claude Code study found structure doesn't matter for a single instruction — and that residency is weak.** McMillan (2026; 1,650 sessions, Sonnet 4.6) finds no effect of CLAUDE.md size (25–500 lines), position, architecture or adjacent contradictions on compliance with one trivial always-applicable MUST-rule, with Bayes-factor support for the size null. But that rule was followed in only ~55–68% of functions, task identity mattered more than any structural choice, and compliance decayed across the session (OR 0.944 per generated function). Read together: within 500 lines, *file size is not the lever; residency itself is a weak enforcement mechanism*. Nothing here covers Praxion's 60–90K regime. [certainty: low-med — single source, unreplicated; size ceiling 500 lines]

### Q3 Repository context files (AGENTS.md / CLAUDE.md)

- **Correctness effects are small in every controlled study.** Gloaguen et al.: −0.5 to −2 pp (LLM-generated), +4 pp mean (developer-written, but not for Claude Code). Khatri: null on Claude Code and Codex (underpowered, MDE >30 pp). Lulla: correctness not measured. No study shows a large gain. [certainty: med — two correctness studies, one underpowered]
- **Cost effects disagree in direction.** Gloaguen: +19–23% cost, +2.5–3.9 steps (more exploration and testing). Lulla: −28.6% median runtime, −16.6% median output tokens on Codex. Khatri hypothesises the gap comes from task difficulty mix; untested. See Divergence Map.
- **Generated guidance doesn't help.** Gloaguen (generated context files ≤ no file) and SkillsBench (self-generated skills −1.3 pp) are independent traditions. [certainty: high]
- **Resident vs on-demand delivery of the *same* repository knowledge:** Khatri's "selective" arm (files on disk + a one-line pointer) matched always-on correctness on Claude Code and cut cache-creation tokens in 11/11 tasks. Vercel's first-party eval found the opposite for unfamiliar APIs: an 8KB resident index scored 100% vs 53–79% for an on-demand skill that went uninvoked 56% of the time. The reconciling variable appears to be **whether the agent reliably fetches when it needs to**. [certainty: low-med — one underpowered preprint vs one vendor eval]
- **Size regime gap:** studied files average 641 words (max 2,003). Praxion's memory-file payload (~29K tokens) is roughly 10–40× the largest studied file. No study measures context files in that regime. [certainty: high that the gap exists]
- **Content guidance from the authors:** "describe only minimal requirements, such as specific tooling" (Gloaguen v1); "repository overviews … don't help", files are useful for "non-standard coding practices" (Gloaguen v3). McMillan suggests position and format are not the levers. Converging content test: keep what the model would not do by default; drop what it already knows or can discover. [certainty: med — one study's content analysis plus vendor guidance ("smallest set of high-signal tokens")] Descriptive work (Agent READMEs) shows files accrete by small additions — the failure mode Praxion's own payload exhibits.

### Q4 Just-in-time guidance selection

- **Focused, curated procedural guidance helps — when it reaches the model.** SkillsBench: +16.2 pp mean across 7 configurations including four Claude models; small model + skills beats large model without. Dynamic Cheatsheet, Memp and ACE show the same direction for curated or self-curated procedural memory. [certainty: high for direction; SWE-domain gain is the smallest (+4.5 pp)]
- **More units and broader units erode the gain.** SkillsBench: 1–3 skills +17.8/+18.6 pp vs 4+ skills +5.9 pp; "comprehensive" skills −2.9 pp while "detailed" and "compact" both help. SkillReducer: removing non-actionable text +2.8%. SkillSeek: beyond ~3 retrieved candidates adds little. [certainty: med-high — three independent groups]
- **Selection over a flat catalogue degrades with size and confusability; one level of routing restores it.** Li (2026): >90% to 20 skills, ~20% at 200 (2024 models), hierarchy +37–40 pts for 4o-mini. He et al. (2026): one routing level helps at scale, a second never helps. RAG-MCP and Anthropic Tool Search (prior spike) agree on direction. [certainty: med — magnitudes from older/smaller models]
- **The retrieval/invocation step is where JIT leaks.** Vercel (56% non-invocation), Liu et al. (gains approach baseline under realistic retrieval), SkillsBench (Codex "frequently neglects" skills), and Praxion's own telemetry (12 on-demand `Skill` calls in 18,042 rows, from the brief) all point at the trigger, not the content. [certainty: med-high — three external traditions plus internal data]
- **Organisation changes use.** SkillJuror: progressive-disclosure layout +4.1% pass, 3× resource use. He et al.: PD "buys context, not intelligence". [certainty: med]
- **No study found** that evaluates keyword- or classifier-triggered *rule* injection (hook-style) against resident rules. PolicyGuard/ToolGuard-style work compiles policy into per-tool checks rather than retrieving prose — adjacent, not the same. [gap]

### Q5 Compression and prompt ablation

- **Token-dropping compression is untested on rules and unreliable on instructions.** LLMLingua's evaluations are on reasoning, chat and documents. The only RCT on orchestration instructions (Johnson & Lee, Sonnet 4.5) found moderate compression saves 28% cost but aggressive compression saves nothing (outputs expand), and it measured quality only as similarity. [certainty: low — single RCT, no correctness metric]
- **Structure-aware pruning is better supported than lexical compression.** SkillReducer (separate core rules from on-demand supplements: −39% body, +2.8% quality) and SkillsBench (comprehensive hurts, compact fine) agree that cutting *non-actionable or off-task* text preserves quality. [certainty: med]
- **"Less is more" is about relevance, not brevity.** ACE (ICLR 2026) shows that compressing *relevant* domain knowledge into terse summaries loses accuracy ("brevity bias"); SkillsBench's "detailed" skills do best. GEPA's optimised prompts are up to 9.2× shorter than MIPROv2's, but partly because demos are dropped. Reconciliation: prune what doesn't apply; don't summarise what does. [certainty: med]

### Q6 Sub-agent context isolation

- **Isolation buys parallel token budget for breadth-first work, and hurts tightly-coupled sequential work.** Anthropic (vendor: token usage explains 80% of BrowseComp variance; +90.2% on research; "not a fit for most coding tasks") and Kim et al. (academic: +80.8% decomposable to −70% sequential) are two independent traditions agreeing. [certainty: med-high]
- **Note what "token usage explains variance" means:** it is *useful working tokens* spent searching, not resident overhead. It cannot be read as "more resident context helps". [certainty: high — follows from the setup]
- **What a sub-agent should inherit: no ablation exists.** Anthropic's guidance is a task brief (objective, output format, tools/sources, boundaries); Cognition argues for full traces. Neither measured inheriting a global rulebook. Praxion's ~21K inherited rules per spawn is unexamined by the literature. [gap]
- **Alternative route to savings:** Li (2026) cut tokens 53.7% by compiling multi-agent into single-agent-with-skills at similar accuracy (small benchmarks, GPT-4o-mini).

### Q7 Survey framing

Mei et al.'s taxonomy maps Praxion's levers cleanly: *context retrieval & generation* ↔ JIT skill/rule selection; *context processing* ↔ compression/restructuring; *context management* ↔ memory hierarchy, compaction, caching; *multi-agent systems* ↔ sub-agent isolation. The practitioner shorthand "write / select / compress / isolate" (LangChain, Anthropic) is the same partition. [LangChain framing not fetched — ASSUMPTION]

## Gaps Praxion must measure itself

1. **Conditional-rulebook adherence vs resident rule volume.** No study measures adherence to rules that apply *only in some situations* while many others sit resident. Praxion would need: a set of rule-triggering tasks (commit, ADR write, worktree, diagram), adherence scored per rule, with the rest of the rulebook resident vs absent.
2. **Quality at 60–90K of guidance on current Opus/Sonnet in agentic coding.** All length studies use filler, haystacks or synthetic corpora, older models, or 800K. Praxion needs an A/B on its own tasks: full startup payload vs pruned payload, same tasks, ≥120–200 tasks or paired designs to detect ~10 pp (Khatri's power analysis).
3. **Trigger reliability for each delivery mechanism in Claude Code.** Model-initiated `Skill` calls, hook/keyword-injected rules, `@`-imports, and path-scoped rules: how often does the right guidance arrive when needed? Vercel's 56% non-invocation is a single vendor number on one framework.
4. **What sub-agents should inherit.** No ablation of global rules vs task brief vs full trace. Praxion's ~21K per spawn and 240K of preloaded skill bodies are the natural experiment.
5. **Concurrently-applicable instruction count per action** in real Praxion sessions — the quantity IFScale-type results actually bear on.
6. **Within-session decay** (McMillan OR 0.944/function) vs reminder injection: does re-surfacing a rule at the point of use (hook) beat resident placement at session start?
7. **Confusability inside Praxion's own catalogue** (near-duplicate rule/skill descriptions) — the distractor class the literature says matters most.
8. **Cost accounting with caching:** most papers report raw tokens or $ without prompt caching; Praxion's real cost is cache_creation + cache_read. Quality effects are independent of caching; cost effects are not.

## Changes since the 2026-09-23 spike

- The prior spike's scope was tool/skill *selection*. This lens adds three literatures it did not cover: **instruction density** (IFScale, ManyIFEval, AgentIF, Eliav, Anand & Chattaraj, Qi 2601.22047, Control Illusion), **repository context files** (Gloaguen, Lulla, Khatri, McMillan, Agent READMEs, Vercel), and **skill effectiveness at scale** (SkillsBench, Liu et al., SkillJuror, He et al., SkillReducer, 138K SKILL.md).
- Published after 2026-09-23: **Gloaguen et al. v3** (2026-09-29; abstract now says overviews don't help and files earn their place by stating *non-standard* practices — the content criterion most relevant to pruning Praxion's payload), **SkillSeek** (arXiv 2609.38822, 2026-09-30; small top-k suffices, metadata indexing is enough) and the v2 of *The Harness Effect* (2607.06906, 2026-10-05; harness-only changes cut tokens/task 38% at quality parity on six models — vendor-authored, abstract only).
- The prior spike's directional conclusion — "removing candidates improves accuracy as well as cost" — is **reinforced** for selection (Li, SkillSeek, SkillsBench 4+ skills) but **qualified** for delivery: the new context-file and Vercel evidence shows on-demand delivery can lose more through non-invocation than it gains through less distraction.
